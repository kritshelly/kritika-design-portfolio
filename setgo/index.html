01 / THE OPPORTUNITY

Driving is familiar.
This car isn’t.

A rental handover introduces a new set of controls at the exact moment a driver wants to leave.

SETGO explores a calmer beginning: preview the essentials on a phone, locate them in the parked car, and connect only when you choose.

Reduce the guesswork.

Recognition is easier when the relevant information is visible in context.

DESIGN RESPONSE

Pair plain-language instructions with a cockpit diagram. Give each control its own moment.

03 / CHOICE

Not everyone needs
every step.

Progressive disclosure reveals detail when it becomes relevant.

DESIGN RESPONSE

04 / TRUST

Connection should
never be a mystery.

Volvo’s pairing instructions ask drivers to compare the codes on their phone and car.

DESIGN RESPONSE

Explicit code confirmation, a mismatch state, and the option to continue without connecting.

Volvo / Phone pairing ↗

These are design inferences from secondary sources, not findings from driver interviews or measured usability outcomes.

07 / THE MOMENTS BETWEEN

Designed beyond
the happy path.

01

Permission to skip.

Pairing is optional. Choosing not to connect still leads to a usable home screen.

02

A way back.

A code mismatch explains the issue and offers both retry and continue without a phone.

03

An explicit pause.

The setup tutorial becomes unavailable outside Park and can resume when parked again.

04

A temporary session.

Session removal explains its scope. It does not imply a factory reset of the vehicle.

09 / THE DESIGN POSITION

Familiarity is
a product feature.

SETGO treats the handover as an experience of its own: prepare, orient, choose and leave with clarity.

The result is a connected HMI and mobile concept with optional paths, explicit system states and recoverable errors. The work demonstrates a design direction; real-world usability and vehicle integration remain unvalidated.
