Where does timing break down?

03 / EVIDENCE INTO DECISIONS

What the conversations changed.

P1 → ACTUAL DEPARTURE

Start with remaining tasks.

Cleaning extends the restaurant worker’s shift. The departure check begins with readiness to leave, so a scheduled finish is never presented as a confirmed departure.

P2 → THE WHOLE JOURNEY

Make the connection visible.

The retail worker already checks live departures. The concept keeps that habit and prompts a review of the onward connection through a locally available transport source.

P1 → CONDITIONAL FALLBACK

A colleague is an option, not a guarantee.

A shared lift helps only when finishing times align. Any coordination must be voluntary and confirmed directly; the concept does not assume a ride is available.

05 / STRATEGIC CHOICE

A smaller intervention.
A closer fit.

The initial idea centred on a hosted pause space. The conversations shifted the priority toward departure timing and connections. The workplace handover is now the core concept.

INITIAL DIRECTION

A place after work.

A separate host would offer somewhere to wait, with its own space, staffing and availability commitments.

CURRENT DIRECTION

A decision before departure.

An existing closing process becomes the intervention point. The supervisor can address work allocation; the worker can keep an arrangement that already works.

DIRECTIONFIT WITH THE ACCOUNTSTRADE-OFFDECISION
Hosted pause spaceNeither worker requested a separate venue.An extra destination, staffing and operating costs.Deprioritised; needs distinct evidence of demand.
Employer transportCould address the trip itself.Routes, funding and demand need local investigation.An alternative if departure coordination is insufficient.
Workplace departure checkAddresses closing overruns and builds on an existing departure-checking habit.Cannot change transport availability or guarantee a connection.Develop as the first prototype.

The strategic choice is to act where the workplace has influence: task allocation, closing communication and pickup coordination. Transport availability remains outside the service’s control.

LEVERAGE POINT

Act while work can change.

The workplace controls how closing tasks are allocated. A concern raised only at the exit is less actionable than one raised during allocation. The concept therefore sits inside the closing process.

MINIMUM INTERVENTION

A checklist before a platform.

The base offer is a clear task owner and expected departure. Journey support appears only when relevant. A paper prompt and direct conversation can deliver the core logic without a new app.

STRATEGIC LIMIT

Escalate structural barriers.

If routes, fares or staffing are the main constraint, better prompts cannot solve them. Employer transport, roster changes or staffing decisions belong with management.

Decision criteria · why this direction

The options were compared qualitatively against five criteria: fit with the two accounts, employer control, added worker effort, operating dependencies and access without a smartphone. These criteria support a scoped concept decision; they are not a weighted score or proof of superiority.

The departure-support layer is retained only where a journey concern changes the action a supervisor can take. Otherwise, the closing checklist is the simpler service.

after

BEYOND CLOSING TIME / A CLEAR NEXT STEP

An optional journey check within closing-task allocation.

The closing checklist remains the base process. A worker can raise a journey concern when it requires action, or finish quickly when the arrangement already works. The shift lead owns task allocation and responds when closing overruns. Existing transport information supports the journey check; any colleague coordination remains optional.

Get ready→Check the journey→Choose a fallback
01

Actual readiness

Make unfinished closing tasks visible to the shift lead before treating the worker as ready to leave.

02

Journey review

Check the first departure and any connection using information available for the worker’s city and route.

03

An agreed fallback

When the plan changes, identify the next available option. A colleague’s lift requires direct agreement; an unavailable option stays unavailable.

09 / OPERATING LOGIC

Start with the workplace.
Account for the effort.

The narrower concept removes a separate host from the initial service. Its feasibility depends on staff time, ownership and useful local information.

01

Time and training

Cost the shift lead’s preparation, worker orientation and the departure check within paid working time.

02

Information upkeep

Assign someone to maintain the local information references and the paper version. Live data access is not assumed.

03

Value to assess

Compare effort with worker usefulness and clearer handling of closing overruns. Retention, safety and commute savings are not measured outcomes.

A Noida operating budget requires workplace conditions and actual staff-time estimates. No cost savings or willingness to fund have been established.

MANAGEMENT CASE

One process, explicit ownership.

The employer would own the closing process and fund any additional coordination time. The proposed value is clearer handling of delays; reduced turnover or travel costs are not assumed.

RESOURCE MODEL

Count the full workload.

Operating cost = paid coordination time + training + information upkeep + materials or tooling. Any transport subsidy is a separate management decision, not a hidden promise in the interface.

ADOPTION SEQUENCE

Start with the familiar process.

Introduce the readiness prompt in the existing checklist, define supervisor responses, then add digital status only where it helps coordination. Dedicated software is not a prerequisite.

10 / DESIGN BOUNDARIES

Support without shifting the burden.

Closing routinely runs late

The employer reviews task allocation and staffing. The departure check must not become an excuse to normalise unpaid overtime.

A lift is not available

Colleague coordination stays voluntary. Nothing appears confirmed until the people involved agree directly.

Transport information is missing

The interface shows the uncertainty. It does not invent a departure or promise that a connection is possible.

A phone is unavailable

A paper prompt and direct shift-lead conversation preserve the readiness check. They cannot replace live transport information.

Support becomes surveillance

No home-address collection, worker tracking or compulsory arrival confirmation. Travel choices do not become a performance measure.

The real barrier is transport coverage

A workplace check cannot create a missing service. Employer transport or scheduling changes become alternative directions.

11 / DESIGN OUTCOME

A small intervention.
A clearer responsibility.

The most consequential decision was reducing the service’s scope. After Hours places the journey concern where work can still be reorganised, while keeping reliable travel arrangements outside unnecessary steps.

STRATEGIC REFRAME

Begin before the door.

The direction moved from a separate waiting venue to closing-task allocation. The workplace can address unfinished work and clarify who responds before departure.

SERVICE DESIGN

Give the request an owner.

The blueprint assigns responsibility to the closing supervisor. Draft, pending and response states distinguish asking for help from having a task or ride arranged.

INTERACTION DESIGN

Preserve what already works.

Workers with a reliable arrangement have a short path. Others can review their journey or raise a task concern. Personal lifts and employer pickups remain distinct.

Concept deliverables: service proposition, decision rationale, journey, operating roles, blueprint and interactive prototype. These describe the design outcome, not measured service impact.
